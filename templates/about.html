1. Problem statement

People who communicate in sign language often cannot be understood by those who do not know it, which creates barriers in healthcare, education, public services and everyday life. SignConnect explores a low-cost, camera-only way to bridge that gap for a small, clearly defined vocabulary.

2. How the system works

  1. CaptureWebcam frame via the browser
  2. DetectMediaPipe Hands finds the hand
  3. Extract21 landmarks → 63 values
  4. NormalizeWrist-centred, size-independent
  5. ClassifyTrained scikit-learn model
  6. ThresholdBelow 70% → “Unknown”
  7. SpeakSentence + text-to-speech

4. Machine-learning methodology

Features

Landmarks are translated so the wrist is the origin and scaled by the largest hand extent, so results do not depend on where the hand is or how close it is.

Model selection

Random Forest, SVM (RBF), KNN and an MLP neural network are trained on a stratified 80/20 split and compared with 5-fold cross-validation. The best F1 score wins.

Reliability

A confidence threshold rejects uncertain predictions, and a word is added only after it is stable over several consecutive frames.

5. Dataset

The dataset is recorded by the user on the Train page (or ml/collect_data.py) and stored locally as normalized landmarks, never as images.

7. Limitations & responsible use

  • Gestures are project-defined, single-frame, one-hand shapes – not a full ASL/ISL translator, and motion-based signs are not supported.
  • Accuracy depends on the data you record: lighting, camera, hand and number of people.
  • Not for medical or safety-critical decisions; always confirm important messages with the person.
  • Privacy: frames are processed on this computer and are not stored or uploaded.
